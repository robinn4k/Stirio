             DRY cocktail is one with very little or no
   •         sweetening in it. Cocktails should always
             be made in a glass with fine ice. The finer
   the ice, the colder the drink. Cocktails must be
   drunk at once. They are not made to be bottled,
   and will lose much of their tang and flavor if you
   try to do so.

      A mixing glass holds 12 ounces, 6 jiggers, or 24
   tablespoonfuls. The jigger referred to in this book
   holds 2 ounces, or 4 tablespoonfuls. A pony holds
   I ounce, or half a jigger, or 2 tablespoonfuls.

      A maraschino cherry or small green olive is often
   dropped into the cocktail. Some people also prefer
   pickled onions. This is never done, however, unless
   the drinker desires it.

     Angostura bitters are often used in place of
   Boker's bitters, but never more than half the
   quantity.

     The correct highball glass holds 6 ounces, and ~
   pony of the liquor is served with the highball.
