" an iced drink made of spirits, mixed with bitters,
sugar and some aromatic flavouring."
This description hardly does justice to the peculiar
fascination lurking within each glass. Rather would
we dwell on its legendary origin which hints at a
more potent charm.
The Toltec noble, by whom it was originally
discovered, is said to have sent it to the King, by
the hand of his daughter Xochtil. The King, falling
in love with the maiden, named the magic liquor
" Xoc-tl " — a        name    perpetuated    in the   word
" Cocktail."
A more modern theory of the manner in which the
Cocktail got Its name concerns a serving-maid, a
cock-fighting squire and a young lieutenant.
The squire called for drinks to celebrate the
restoration of one          of his finest   fighting cocks,
whereupon the maid mixed a special drink wherewith
to toast " the cock's tail ! "
It is interesting to note that the success of this, the
